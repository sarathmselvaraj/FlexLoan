The Math of Mortgage Refinancing: When Does a 0.50% Drop Make Sense?

By David Sterling · Refinancing Specialist •6 min read•Aug 2026

Mortgage refinancing — or a balance transfer — is one of the most powerful tools available to homeowners. But it comes with hidden costs that can eat up your savings. The key question isn't "Can I get a lower rate?" — it's "Does the lower rate actually save me money after all costs?"

In this guide, we walk through the complete financial calculus of refinancing, including the breakeven period formula that every borrower must understand before signing a balance transfer application.

⚡ Key Takeaways

  • ✓ A 0.50% rate reduction is worthwhile only if you plan to stay in the loan for 4+ more years.
  • ✓ Processing fees + legal costs typically total ₹30,000–₹80,000 for refinancing.
  • ✓ The Breakeven Period (BEP) formula tells you exactly when savings overcome upfront costs.
  • ✓ Refinancing within the first 5 years of a long-tenure loan delivers maximum benefit.

1. Understanding the True Cost of Refinancing

When you transfer your home loan from Bank A to Bank B, you're essentially taking a new loan to pay off the old one. This triggers a set of one-time costs that most borrowers underestimate:

📋 Typical Refinancing Costs
  • Processing Fee (new bank)0.25–0.5% of loan
  • Legal Verification Fee₹5,000–₹15,000
  • Valuation Fee₹3,000–₹8,000
  • MOD Stamp Duty0.1–0.2% of loan
  • MODT Registration₹2,000–₹10,000
  • Typical Total₹30,000–₹80,000
✅ What You Gain
  • • Lower monthly EMI (immediate cash flow relief)
  • • Reduced total interest burden over tenure
  • • Ability to re-negotiate tenure (shorter/longer)
  • • Better bank service quality and features
  • • Access to top-up loans at lower rates
  • • Potential credit score improvement opportunity

2. The Breakeven Period Formula

The most important calculation in refinancing is your Breakeven Period (BEP) — how many months it takes for your monthly savings to recover the upfront refinancing costs.

Breakeven Period Formula
BEP = Total Costs ÷ Monthly EMI Savings
If BEP < Remaining Tenure → Refinancing makes sense
📊 Example Calculation
Outstanding loan balance₹40 Lakh
Current rate → New rate9.5% → 9.0%
Remaining tenure18 years
Current EMI₹39,024
New EMI₹37,498
Monthly Savings₹1,526/month
Total Refinancing Costs₹52,000
Breakeven Period34 months (~3 years)
Decision✅ REFINANCE — 18 yr remaining > 3 yr BEP

3. The Golden Rules of Refinancing

✅ Good Times to Refinance
  • • Rate difference ≥ 0.5% and 10+ years remaining
  • • Your credit score improved significantly
  • • You want to switch fixed → floating (rate cut cycle)
  • • You need a top-up loan at lower rates
  • • Current bank refuses to match competitor rates
❌ Bad Times to Refinance
  • • Less than 5 years remaining on tenure
  • • Rate difference < 0.25% (costs exceed savings)
  • • You plan to sell the property within 2 years
  • • Your credit score has dropped recently
  • • You're in a fixed-rate lock-in period with penalties

4. Pro Tip: Negotiate With Your Existing Bank First

Before applying to a new lender, always request a rate reset with your current bank. Most banks will match or closely approach a competitor's offer to retain your business — especially if you've been a 3+ year customer with clean repayment history. This costs you nothing (no processing fees, no documentation) and can achieve the same rate reduction instantly.
