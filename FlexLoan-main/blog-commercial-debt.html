Commercial Debt Syndication: Structuring SMB Working Capital Lines

By Elena Vance · Chief Financial Analyst •9 min read•Jul 2026

For small and mid-sized businesses (SMBs), accessing institutional working capital is one of the most critical — and most misunderstood — financial challenges. Single-lender credit limits, high collateral requirements, and rigid repayment structures often leave growth-oriented businesses starved of the capital they need.

Commercial debt syndication offers a powerful alternative: spreading your credit requirement across multiple lenders simultaneously, unlocking larger facilities at better pricing. This guide breaks down exactly how to structure it.

⚡ Key Takeaways

  • ✓ Debt syndication allows SMBs to borrow ₹5–250 Cr across 3–8 lenders simultaneously.
  • ✓ Multi-lender structures typically unlock 20–40% lower interest spreads vs. single-lender facilities.
  • ✓ Mezzanine + senior debt blends can cover 90%+ of capital needs with no equity dilution.
  • ✓ A strong IM (Information Memorandum) is the single most critical document in syndication.

1. What Is Debt Syndication?

Debt syndication is the process where a single borrower raises a large credit facility that is funded by a consortium of multiple lenders — banks, NBFCs, institutional investors, and private credit funds. Each lender takes a portion (tranche) of the total loan, sharing the risk while the borrower benefits from a larger, more flexible facility.

A Lead Arranger (typically an advisory firm like FlexiLoan) structures the deal, prepares the Information Memorandum, markets it to the lender consortium, and negotiates unified terms — simplifying the process dramatically for the borrower.

🏦 Typical Syndicate Structure for ₹30 Cr Facility
Lead Bank (State Bank / HDFC) ₹12 Cr — 40%
Co-Lender Bank (Axis / ICICI) ₹9 Cr — 30%
NBFC (Bajaj Finance / Piramal) ₹6 Cr — 20%
Mezzanine / Private Credit Fund ₹3 Cr — 10%

2. The Three Capital Stack Layers

🏛️
Senior Debt
First lien, lowest cost
Rate: 9–11%
Secured by primary assets. Banks & large NBFCs. Priority repayment in default.
📊
Mezzanine Debt
Second lien, mid-cost
Rate: 14–18%
Unsecured or secondary collateral. Private funds. Subordinate to senior in waterfall.
🚀
Subordinated / PIK
High yield, flexible
Rate: 18–24%
Payment-in-kind interest. Last in waterfall. Used for growth capital with high risk tolerance.

3. The Information Memorandum (IM) — Your Most Critical Document

The Information Memorandum is a comprehensive financial dossier presented to potential lenders. It's the equivalent of a business plan for debt financing — and its quality directly determines the interest rate you're offered and whether tier-1 banks participate.

A well-crafted IM typically runs 60–100 pages and covers: company overview, management bios, 3-year audited financials, cash flow projections, collateral schedule, debt waterfall structure, use-of-funds breakdown, and risk mitigation factors.

📋 IM Section Checklist

✓ Executive Summary (2 pages)
✓ Business Overview & Market Position
✓ 3-Year Audited P&L + Balance Sheet
✓ 5-Year Revenue Projections (Base + Stress)
✓ Collateral Schedule with Valuations
✓ Debt Structure & Repayment Waterfall
✓ Management Team CVs
✓ Risk Factors & Mitigation

4. Eligibility: Does Your Business Qualify?

Debt syndication is typically available to businesses with annual revenues of ₹10 Crore or more. However, with the right advisory and credit enhancement structures, even ₹5 Cr+ revenue businesses can access syndicated facilities.
